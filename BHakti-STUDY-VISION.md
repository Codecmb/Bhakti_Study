# BHAKTI STUDY — MASTER VISION & DEVELOPMENT GUIDE

## 1. PURPOSE

Bhakti Study is a comprehensive study environment designed to help students progressively understand, study, investigate, connect, and master Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava scripture and siddhānta.

Bhakti Study should be:

- Self-contained
- Accessible
- Didactic
- Modular
- Precise
- Verifiable
- Expandable
- Non-redundant

Core teaching principle:

> Explain, explain, explain.
> Keep explanations short, concise, precise, and didactic.
> Do not unnecessarily assume that the student already understands a concept.

---

## 2. ENGLISH-FIRST, MULTILINGUAL-READY

Bhakti Study V1 is English-first.

English is the primary interface and study language because the available authoritative study resources are substantially more extensive in English.

However, the architecture must be multilingual-ready from the beginning.

Spanish and other languages should later be added as language layers without creating separate applications or duplicating the academic architecture.

Interface language and source language are separate concepts.

Every source should preserve its actual language and provenance.

Translations must be identified when applicable as:

- Original
- Published translation
- Academy translation
- Other clearly identified translation

An Academy translation must never be presented as an officially published translation.

---

## 3. SELF-CONTAINED FIRST

Bhakti Study should provide everything reasonably necessary for serious study inside the application.

External websites expand the student's research.

They should not be required for ordinary study when the material already exists internally.

Principle:

> Bhakti Study first. The Internet is an expansion layer, not the foundation.

---

## 4. MODULAR ARCHITECTURE

Major programs and systems must remain independently operable.

Examples include:

- Bhakti Śāstrī
- Bhakti Vaibhava
- Bhakti Vedānta
- Bhakti Sārvabhauma
- Ṣaṭ Sandarbhas
- Library
- Study Questions
- Notes
- References
- Progress
- Search
- Imports

Modules may communicate and share resources through stable references.

One module should not unnecessarily depend upon another module being loaded, completed, or active.

Principle:

> Independent modules working together in parallel.

---

## 5. NO REDUNDANCY

Avoid unnecessary duplication throughout Bhakti Study.

A source should have one canonical representation whenever practical.

Do not duplicate a book, verse, commentary, explanation, question, or dataset simply because several courses use it.

Example:

Bhagavad-gītā 2.13 should have a canonical identity.

A Bhakti Śāstrī lesson, question, note, concept, or another course can reference that identity rather than creating another copy of BG 2.13.

Principle:

> One source of truth, many uses.

A correction to canonical data should propagate wherever that data is referenced.

Modularity should be achieved through references, not unnecessary copies.

---

## 6. SOURCE PROVENANCE AND ATTRIBUTION

Bhakti Study must clearly distinguish authors and sources.

Material that is not directly from Śrīla Prabhupāda must never be presented in a way that could reasonably cause the student to believe that it is his statement.

The same rule applies to every author.

When available, source records should identify:

- Author
- Work
- Chapter
- Verse
- Section
- Page or location
- Edition
- Language
- Content type
- Internal source
- External source

Important voices may include:

- Śrīla Prabhupāda
- Jīva Gosvāmī
- Bhānu Swami
- Hridayananda Goswami Maharaja
- Other Gauḍīya ācāryas and commentators
- ISKCON educational institutions
- Academic authors
- Comparative philosophy sources

Their voices must not be silently merged.

---

## 7. QUOTATION, PARAPHRASE, AND SYNTHESIS

Bhakti Study must distinguish between:

- Direct quotation
- Translation
- Paraphrase
- Commentary
- Summary
- Bhakti Study synthesis

A direct quotation should be traceable to its source whenever possible.

A paraphrase must not be displayed as a quotation.

When Bhakti Study combines information from multiple sources, it should be clearly identified as:

**Bhakti Study Synthesis**

The supporting sources should remain inspectable.

If sufficient evidence is unavailable, Bhakti Study should say so rather than manufacture an answer.

---

## 8. INTERNAL LIBRARY

Students should have direct access to the books required for their studies.

Questions, explanations, concepts, and references should connect directly to the relevant internal source whenever available.

Ideal study path:

Question
→ Explanation
→ Scriptural Reference
→ Verse or Passage
→ Commentary
→ Chapter
→ Book
→ Related References

The student should not have to leave Bhakti Study simply to inspect a source already contained in the library.

---

## 9. DRAG-AND-DROP IMPORTS

Bhakti Study should allow students and authorized maintainers to add study material easily.

Priority formats:

- PDF
- EPUB
- TXT

Additional formats may be supported through modular importers.

Importing a resource should not unnecessarily alter the original file.

When possible, Bhakti Study should identify or request:

- Title
- Author
- Edition
- Language
- Source type
- Chapters
- Verses
- Sections
- Publication information
- Provenance

Imported resources should be indexed for study without unnecessary duplication.

---

## 10. PERSONAL LIBRARY

Student-imported materials must remain distinguishable from Academy-provided or authoritative source collections.

A personal resource may participate in:

- Search
- Notes
- Questions
- References
- Research
- Concept exploration

Importing a resource does not automatically make it an authoritative doctrinal source.

The interface should make source status clear.

---

## 11. STUDY QUESTIONS

Students should be able to:

- Answer provided questions
- Create their own questions
- Write answers
- Save reflections
- Connect questions to sources
- Add supporting references
- Revisit questions
- Turn questions into review material

A question should not function only as an examination item.

It should become an entrance into deeper study.

---

## 12. STUDY INSPECTOR

When studying or answering a question, Bhakti Study should be able to open a contextual Study Inspector.

Depending on available sources, it may show:

- Question
- Student answer
- Concise explanation
- Related verses
- Śrīla Prabhupāda's commentary
- Bhānu Swami's explanations
- Hridayananda Goswami Maharaja's explanations
- Jīva Gosvāmī and other ācārya references
- Related concepts
- Personal notes
- Cross-references
- External research
- Comparative philosophy

Every source must retain its identity.

The Study Inspector should connect information, not erase distinctions between authors.

---

## 13. "WHERE DOES THIS COME FROM?"

Every significant explanation should support provenance inspection whenever possible.

The student should be able to ask:

> Where does this come from?

Bhakti Study should then show available information such as:

- Who said it
- In which work
- Exact reference
- Edition
- Whether it is a quotation, translation, paraphrase, commentary, or synthesis
- Where the original source can be inspected

Bibliographic transparency is part of the student's education.

---

## 14. CONCEPTUAL KNOWLEDGE MAP

Bhakti Study should help students understand relationships between concepts.

Example:

jīva
→ ātman
→ taṭasthā-śakti
→ mamaivāṁśa
→ bhedābheda
→ māyā
→ karma
→ transmigration
→ liberation
→ bhakti

Concepts should connect to supporting:

- Verses
- Passages
- Commentaries
- Questions
- Courses
- Books
- Notes
- Related concepts

The knowledge map should be generated through references rather than redundant copies of source material.

---

## 15. GLOBAL SEARCH

Students should be able to search the available study environment from one place.

Internal search has priority.

Search may include:

- Scriptures
- Verses
- Commentaries
- Sandarbhas
- Books
- Questions
- Courses
- Concepts
- Personal notes
- Imported materials

A separate option may allow:

**Expand Research**

This can search or open appropriate external resources.

---

## 16. EXTERNAL RESEARCH

External resources supplement the internal library.

Useful resources may include:

- Vanipedia
- Bhaktivedanta Vedabase
- VIHE
- ISKCON educational resources
- Relevant GBC and Sastric Advisory Council resources
- Radha.name
- Appropriate Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava resources
- Academic resources
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
- Other serious philosophy resources

External resources must be identified by source and category.

Not every source has the same authority.

---

## 17. SOURCE CATEGORIES

Bhakti Study should distinguish categories such as:

1. Primary scripture
2. Śrīla Prabhupāda
3. Gauḍīya ācāryas and recognized commentaries
4. ISKCON educational material
5. Supplementary research
6. Comparative philosophy
7. Personal library material
8. Bhakti Study synthesis

These categories assist navigation and attribution.

They must not be used to silently change or rewrite the original source.

---

## 18. COMPARATIVE PHILOSOPHY

Bhakti Study may support broader philosophical education while keeping Gauḍīya siddhānta clearly identifiable.

Possible comparative sources include:

- Plato
- Aristotle
- Plotinus
- Augustine
- Thomas Aquinas
- Descartes
- Spinoza
- Leibniz
- Kant
- Hegel
- Schopenhauer

Comparisons may help students investigate subjects such as:

- Self and soul
- Epistemology and pramāṇa
- Causality
- Personalism
- Impersonalism
- Metaphysics
- Consciousness
- God
- Ethics

Comparison should clarify differences and similarities without artificially merging philosophical systems.

---

## 19. PROGRESSIVE DEPTH

Bhakti Study should accommodate both beginning and advanced students.

A useful progression is:

Short Explanation
→ Detailed Explanation
→ Scriptural Evidence
→ Commentary
→ Cross-References
→ Advanced Research
→ External Resources

A beginner should not be unnecessarily overwhelmed.

An advanced student should not be trapped in superficial explanations.

---

## 20. INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

Bhakti Study must not invent information merely to provide an answer.

When the available corpus does not adequately support a conclusion, the system should indicate:

**Not Found / Insufficient Evidence**

It may then offer paths such as:

- Search Internal Library
- Search Related Concepts
- Inspect Related Sources
- Expand Research

Uncertainty should be visible rather than hidden.

---

## 21. SHARED ENGINES

Whenever practical, use shared systems for:

- Library
- Reader
- Search
- Questions
- References
- Bibliography
- Notes
- Progress
- Imports
- Concept navigation
- Provenance

Do not build unnecessary independent copies of these systems for every course.

Courses consume shared services while remaining independently operable.

---

## 22. PLUG-AND-PLAY EXPANSION

The architecture should make it possible to add:

- A book
- An edition
- A translation
- A commentary
- An author
- A course
- A module
- A question bank
- A reference collection
- A new importer
- A new language

without rebuilding the entire application.

New resources should register with existing systems rather than require duplicated infrastructure.

---

## 23. DEVELOPMENT RULES

Before changing Bhakti Study:

1. Inspect what already exists.
2. Do not assume.
3. Explain before making consequential architectural changes.
4. Check dependencies.
5. Preserve modularity.
6. Avoid redundancy.
7. Make the smallest appropriate change.
8. Preserve existing data.
9. Do not change unrelated UI or functionality.
10. Verify the result.
11. Keep Git changes focused and understandable.
12. Prefer reusable infrastructure over duplicated solutions.
13. Preserve source provenance.
14. Never silently change attribution or authorship.

---

## 24. DESIGN TEST

Before implementing an important feature, ask:

> Does this make Bhakti Study more self-contained, understandable, verifiable, modular, and expandable without creating redundancy or obscuring source provenance?

If the answer is unclear:

**Stop, inspect, explain, then decide.**

---

## 25. PROJECT NORTH STAR

Bhakti Study is not merely a collection of books.

Bhakti Study is not merely a collection of courses.

It is an integrated study environment:

**Academy + Library + Scripture + Questions + Explanations + Research + References + Notes + Progress + Comparative Study + Sources**

Everything should be connected through references.

Everything important should be traceable.

Sources should retain their identity.

Modules should remain modular.

Data should not be unnecessarily duplicated.

The system should explain rather than assume.

The goal is to help the student learn how to study, understand, investigate, connect, and progressively master the subject.
